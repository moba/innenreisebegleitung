# Trauma-Sensitive Coaching with Ego States

Verena König describes why accompaniment outside a therapeutic setting is needed – and what matters in it. She writes in German; the English rendering below is mine and is not an authorised translation.

_“A great many people affected by early trauma suffer from a wide range of complaints that go unrecognised as trauma sequelae for a long time. The spectrum of these symptoms is wide, reaching from problems with self-worth and attachment through physical health issues to anxiety and depression. Many of those affected do not look for support in therapy to begin with, and when they do, they face long waiting times or get no place at all. The places open to them at shorter notice are found in counselling and coaching settings, in self-help groups, or in personal development offerings. In those places – and just as often in therapeutic settings too – any competence in dealing with trauma is frequently missing. That fact sends many of those affected by early and complex trauma on an odyssey lasting years as they go in search of healing, or at least of relief from their complaints. Without trauma-specific support, however, that search cannot come to a successful end._

_One approach to improving the state of care lies in broadening the competence of the helping professions – especially of those who offer accompaniment outside a therapeutic framework. Trauma-sensitive support is a key to easing feelings of powerlessness and overwhelm, and it helps to soften symptoms and to further the integration of trauma._

_The significance of trauma-sensitive accompaniment is still both an overlooked necessity and an overlooked resource. Building up that resource is a challenge, but it also holds a great opportunity._

_The challenge lies in creating frameworks, with low-threshold concepts, in which people living with the consequences of trauma can be accompanied safely and professionally, without blurring the boundary to the therapeutic setting. The great opportunity lies in the fact that those affected gain faster access to lastingly helpful offerings, and that a new perspective on their symptoms and a new understanding of themselves is opened up to them._

_As their capacities for self-regulation are strengthened and they learn to meet their trauma sequelae with a sense of their own agency, their paths of suffering are shortened as well. […]_

_The term »trauma-sensitive« is subject to no clear definition. Inspired by the National Center for Trauma-Informed Care, I use it as follows: to be trauma-sensitive means to know of the existence of trauma, to be able to recognise trauma and its consequences, to be able to deal with them, and to avoid retraumatisation.”_

Verena König: *"Traumasensibles Coaching mit Ego-States –
Wie wir die Arbeit mit Ego-States niedrigschwellig und sicher
im nichttherapeutischen Setting implementieren können"* in: *Ego-State-Therapie: Von den historischen Wurzeln zur innovativen Praxis*, edited by Woltemade Hartman (2026). Quoted from [traum-und-verantwortung.de](https://www.traum-und-verantwortung.de/zitate/traumasensibles-coaching/).
